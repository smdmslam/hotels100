# DMW Hotels 100 — Ranking Methodology

## 1. Purpose

This document defines the preliminary methodology used to assess and rank hotels for the DMW Hotels 100.

The methodology must reflect the product’s distinctive point of view:

* Hotels as guest experiences
* Hotels as luxury products
* Hotels as operating businesses
* Hotels as brands
* Hotels as real-estate assets
* Hotels as subjects of investment and strategic judgement

The calculation provides discipline and comparability. It does not replace informed editorial judgement.

---

## 2. Methodology Status

This is:

**Methodology Version 0.1 — Prototype**

It must be tested against at least seven materially different hotels before being treated as final.

Initial test group:

* St Martins Lane, London
* The May Fair Hotel, London
* Flemings Mayfair, London
* The Athenaeum Hotel, London
* Hôtel Métropole Monte-Carlo
* NH Collection Madrid Eurobuilding
* W Barcelona

The test group is useful because it includes:

* Urban lifestyle hotels
* Mayfair luxury hotels
* Business-led properties
* Resort-style urban hotels
* Independent and branded propositions
* Different room-rate positions
* Different amenity ecosystems

Weights and scoring definitions may be adjusted after this calibration exercise.

---

## 3. Governing Principle

The ranking should reward hotels that combine:

* A compelling guest proposition
* Consistent execution
* Distinctive identity
* Useful and coherent amenities
* Defensible pricing power
* Strategic business logic
* Scarce or valuable asset characteristics
* Long-term relevance

A hotel should not rank highly merely because it is:

* Expensive
* Famous
* Historic
* Newly opened
* Heavily designed
* Part of a prestigious brand
* Located in an expensive market
* Personally liked by the evaluator

The central question is:

> How successfully does this hotel convert its location, property, brand, design, service and amenities into a coherent and defensible luxury-hospitality proposition?

---

## 4. Ranking Dimensions

Each hotel is scored across ten dimensions.

Each dimension receives a score from 0 to 10.

| Dimension                                       |   Weight |
| ----------------------------------------------- | -------: |
| Hospitality proposition and strategic coherence |      15% |
| Service and operating execution                 |      12% |
| Distinctiveness and emotional resonance         |      12% |
| Rooms and functional performance                |      10% |
| Amenities and hospitality ecosystem             |      10% |
| Location and asset scarcity                     |      10% |
| Pricing power and value integrity               |      10% |
| Brand positioning and clientele coherence       |       8% |
| Business-travel effectiveness                   |       7% |
| Long-term resilience and strategic potential    |       6% |
| **Total**                                       | **100%** |

---

# 5. Dimension Definitions

## 5.1 Hospitality Proposition and Strategic Coherence

### Weight

15%

### Question

What is the guest truly buying, and how coherently does the hotel deliver that promise?

### Assessment areas

* Clarity of the guest proposition
* Relationship between location, design, rooms, service and amenities
* Intended clientele
* Consistency between price and product
* Coherence across the guest journey
* Strength of the hotel’s strategic concept
* Whether the hotel knows what it is—and what it is not

### High score

A hotel with a clear, differentiated proposition where the physical asset, service model, brand, amenities and price work together.

### Low score

A hotel with expensive components but no coherent idea, or one whose actual operation contradicts its stated positioning.

---

## 5.2 Service and Operating Execution

### Weight

12%

### Question

How consistently and intelligently does the operation deliver the intended experience?

### Assessment areas

* Arrival
* Front-desk efficiency
* Concierge and doorman engagement
* Housekeeping
* Responsiveness
* Personalisation
* Problem resolution
* Service speed
* Cross-department consistency
* Peak-period performance

### High score

Service is anticipatory, efficient and appropriate to the hotel’s character without becoming intrusive or theatrical.

### Low score

Queues, inconsistent departments, slow responses, poor recovery or a significant gap between brand promise and operating reality.

### Evidence limitation

This dimension is difficult to verify without firsthand experience. Research-only scores must receive a lower confidence classification.

---

## 5.3 Distinctiveness and Emotional Resonance

### Weight

12%

### Question

Does the hotel create a memorable sense of place, identity or emotional response?

### Assessment areas

* Arrival impact
* Atmosphere
* Design identity
* Cultural relevance
* Sense of place
* Memorability
* Originality
* Public-space character
* Avoidance of generic luxury conventions

### High score

The hotel is recognisable, emotionally specific and difficult to confuse with its competitors.

### Low score

The property is polished but generic, derivative or dependent on expensive materials without a meaningful identity.

---

## 5.4 Rooms and Functional Performance

### Weight

10%

### Question

How effectively do the rooms support sleep, work, privacy, comfort and everyday use?

### Assessment areas

* Bed and sleep quality
* Noise
* Spatial efficiency
* Bathroom planning
* Lighting
* Storage
* Workspace
* Technology
* Climate control
* Privacy
* Maintenance
* Views where relevant

### High score

Rooms are comfortable, intuitive and highly functional, even where physical size is limited.

### Low score

Poor planning, inadequate storage, weak lighting, intrusive noise, unreliable technology or design that prioritises appearance over use.

### Important principle

Large rooms do not automatically score more highly. Spatial intelligence matters more than raw area.

---

## 5.5 Amenities and Hospitality Ecosystem

### Weight

10%

### Question

Do the amenities strengthen the hotel’s proposition, guest experience and revenue model?

### Assessment areas

* Restaurants
* Bars
* Breakfast
* Spa
* Gym
* Pool
* Meeting space
* Rooftop
* Gardens or landscape
* Beach access
* Retail
* Cultural programming
* Residences
* Membership
* Events
* Room service
* Transport

### High score

Amenities create a coherent ecosystem, serve resident and non-resident demand and reinforce the hotel’s positioning.

### Low score

Amenities are missing where essential, poorly integrated, operationally weak or present merely as a checklist.

### Important principle

More amenities do not automatically produce a higher score. Relevance and execution matter.

---

## 5.6 Location and Asset Scarcity

### Weight

10%

### Question

How valuable, scarce and strategically useful is the hotel’s physical setting and property?

### Assessment areas

* Location
* Access
* Views
* Heritage
* Architecture
* Planning scarcity
* Site configuration
* Neighbourhood relevance
* Replacement difficulty
* Cultural or natural context
* Development or expansion potential

### High score

An irreplaceable or extremely difficult-to-reproduce property whose location materially strengthens demand and long-term value.

### Low score

A replicable site with weak access, limited destination relevance or a physical asset that works against the intended proposition.

---

## 5.7 Pricing Power and Value Integrity

### Weight

10%

### Question

What does the hotel’s pricing behaviour reveal about demand, positioning and the credibility of its product?

### Assessment areas

* Absolute rate
* Rate premium versus relevant competitors
* Rate resilience
* Discount frequency
* Seasonal curve
* Weekday/weekend behaviour
* Event pricing
* Occupancy context where known
* Value received
* Ability to preserve premium positioning

### High score

The hotel sustains an economically meaningful premium supported by genuine product differentiation and demand.

### Low score

Pricing depends on deep discounting, inconsistent positioning or a brand premium unsupported by the experience.

### Important principle

The most expensive hotel does not automatically receive the highest score.

Pricing power means the ability to sustain a justified premium—not simply post a high rate.

---

## 5.8 Brand Positioning and Clientele Coherence

### Weight

8%

### Question

Does the hotel attract the clientele it intends to serve, and does its brand mean something specific?

### Assessment areas

* Clarity of market position
* Guest profile
* Brand relevance
* Cultural credibility
* Selectivity
* Visibility versus overexposure
* Relationship to parent brand
* Consistency of communications
* Alignment between clientele, atmosphere and price

### High score

The hotel occupies a clear position, attracts a compatible clientele and preserves desirability without becoming generic or overexposed.

### Low score

The brand promise is vague, the clientele conflicts with the intended experience or the property relies on name recognition without maintaining relevance.

---

## 5.9 Business-Travel Effectiveness

### Weight

7%

### Question

How well does the hotel serve the practical and social requirements of an international business traveller?

### Assessment areas

* Location efficiency
* Arrival and departure
* Service speed
* Workspace
* Wi-Fi
* Privacy
* Sleep
* Breakfast practicality
* Meeting facilities
* Informal meeting spaces
* Solo dining
* Transport
* Laundry and pressing
* Short-stay efficiency

### High score

The hotel supports work, rest, meetings and movement without compromising its luxury identity.

### Low score

The property may be visually attractive but creates unnecessary friction for a working traveller.

### Category balance

Resorts and remote properties should not be unfairly penalised for not serving a central-city business purpose.

For hotels where business travel is not a meaningful part of the proposition, assess functional effectiveness for the type of professional or executive use the property reasonably supports.

---

## 5.10 Long-Term Resilience and Strategic Potential

### Weight

6%

### Question

Can the hotel sustain or improve its relevance, economics and asset value over time?

### Assessment areas

* Brand durability
* Physical adaptability
* Reinvestment needs
* Operating resilience
* Scalability
* Site-specific defensibility
* Expansion potential
* Residence or membership opportunities
* Repositioning opportunity
* Exposure to passing trends
* Management and ownership alignment where observable

### High score

A defensible property with enduring demand drivers, manageable reinvestment requirements and credible paths for continued value creation.

### Low score

A trend-dependent concept, vulnerable physical product or unclear investment case requiring disproportionate capital to remain relevant.

---

# 6. Scoring Scale

Use the following scale consistently.

| Score | Meaning                                            |
| ----: | -------------------------------------------------- |
|    10 | Exceptional; among the strongest examples globally |
|     9 | Outstanding; material and defensible strength      |
|     8 | Excellent; clearly above relevant competitors      |
|     7 | Strong; persuasive with identifiable limitations   |
|     6 | Good; credible but not distinctive                 |
|     5 | Adequate; meets expectations without advantage     |
|     4 | Weak; important shortcomings                       |
|     3 | Poor; substantial mismatch or execution failure    |
|     2 | Seriously deficient                                |
|     1 | Fundamentally unsuccessful                         |
|     0 | Absent, inoperable or wholly unsupported           |

Half-point scores are permitted:

```text
7.5
8.5
```

Do not use more than one decimal place.

---

# 7. Weighted Calculation

For each dimension:

```text
Weighted contribution = dimension score × dimension weight
```

Because dimension scores are out of 10 and total weights equal 1.00:

```text
Calculated score out of 100 =
sum of all weighted contributions × 10
```

Example:

```text
Hospitality proposition score: 8.5
Weight: 0.15

8.5 × 0.15 × 10 = 12.75 points
```

The maximum calculated score is 100.

---

# 8. Evidence Confidence

Each dimension must also receive an evidence-confidence level.

## High confidence

Supported by:

* Recent firsthand DMW experience
* Verified property facts
* Strong pricing data
* Multiple reliable sources
* Directly observable characteristics

## Medium confidence

Supported by:

* Reliable structured research
* Official information cross-checked with independent sources
* Consistent guest-feedback patterns
* Partial pricing data
* Older firsthand experience

## Developing confidence

Supported by:

* Limited public information
* Mostly official hotel claims
* Insufficient pricing observations
* Conflicting reports
* No firsthand experience where experience materially affects the dimension

Confidence does not directly change the mathematical score in Version 0.1.

It must instead:

* Remain recorded by dimension
* Be visible internally
* Affect editorial certainty
* Prevent unsupported claims
* Trigger future research or a visit

---

# 9. Research-Only Hotels

A hotel does not require a DMW visit to enter the ranking.

For every hotel entering through structured research:

* Complete all research-supported dimensions
* Mark service and room-experience confidence appropriately
* Separate official claims from independent evidence
* Avoid first-person experiential language
* Do not fabricate sensory observations

Hotels receive no penalty because they have not been visited. This is the normal operating model of the index.

However, uncertainty must be documented and may affect whether the hotel is ready for final publication.

---

# 10. Optional Firsthand Notes

When firsthand experience exists:

* Add an optional dated firsthand note within the permanent profile
* Reassess service, room function, arrival and atmosphere
* Compare firsthand experience with the prior research thesis
* Increase evidence confidence only where the visit genuinely provides evidence
* Record the date and context of the visit

A single stay does not prove perfect operational consistency.

The firsthand note should state:

* Length of stay
* Approximate date
* Travel purpose
* Room category where known
* Approximate paid rate where appropriate

---

# 11. Missing Data Treatment

Do not enter zero merely because information is unavailable.

Use:

```text
null
```

for missing numeric scores until sufficient evidence exists.

A hotel should not receive a final calculated score unless:

* Every dimension has a score
* Each score has an evidence note
* Confidence is assigned
* Major factual conflicts are resolved or disclosed

For prototype display, incomplete rankings must be labelled:

**Illustrative ranking — methodology calibration in progress**

---

# 12. Editorial Adjustment

After the calculated score is produced, DMW may apply an editorial adjustment between:

```text
-5.0 and +5.0 points
```

The adjustment exists because a quantitative model may fail to capture:

* Exceptional strategic coherence
* Severe internal contradiction
* A transformative location
* Unusual historical or cultural importance
* Material recent deterioration
* A temporary anomaly in the data
* A category-balance issue
* An emerging hotel with incomplete historical evidence

Every adjustment must include:

* Adjustment value
* Written reason
* Reviewer
* Date
* Methodology version

The reason must be specific.

Unacceptable reason:

> We simply preferred this hotel.

Acceptable reason:

> The calculated score understates the property’s site-specific scarcity because no individual asset dimension fully captures its combination of protected landscape, limited keys and non-replicable access.

Final score:

```text
Final score = calculated score + editorial adjustment
```

The final score cannot fall below 0 or exceed 100.

---

# 13. Translating Scores into Rank

Hotels are ordered by final score.

If two hotels have the same final score, use the following tie-break sequence:

1. Hospitality proposition and strategic coherence
2. Pricing power and value integrity
3. Service and operating execution
4. Location and asset scarcity
5. Higher overall evidence confidence
6. Editorial review

Scores should normally remain private or secondary.

The public product should emphasise:

* Rank
* DMW judgement
* Distinctions
* Analysis

It should not create false scientific precision by making a score such as `87.4` the dominant public message.

---

# 14. Category and Geographic Balance

The list is global, but it must not use arbitrary quotas.

Do not guarantee a fixed number of hotels by:

* Country
* Continent
* Brand
* Hotel type

However, after mathematical scoring, conduct a portfolio-level review for:

* Overconcentration in a single city
* Overrepresentation of major chains
* Underrepresentation caused by research bias
* Excessive focus on urban hotels
* Excessive focus on ultra-high room rates
* Failure to consider relevant independent properties

This review may trigger further candidate research.

It must not force a weaker hotel into the list solely to satisfy a geographic quota.

---

# 15. Candidate Eligibility

A hotel may enter the candidate universe if it:

* Is operating or has a confirmed near-term opening
* Provides a professionally managed lodging product
* Competes meaningfully in luxury, upper-upscale or strategically distinctive hospitality
* Has sufficient public evidence for assessment
* Offers a proposition relevant to the global index

Eligible forms include:

* Hotels
* Resorts
* Lodges
* Private-island properties
* Wellness retreats
* Hotel and residence ecosystems
* Hotel and members-club hybrids

Generally exclude:

* Private homes without hotel operations
* Short-term rentals
* Pure residential developments
* Restaurants without lodging
* Clubs without meaningful lodging
* Properties unavailable to the public by any route
* Hotels whose information cannot be responsibly verified

---

# 16. New Hotels

A newly opened hotel may be ranked if:

* It has operated long enough for meaningful assessment
* Core amenities are open
* Pricing and service evidence exist
* Opening-period anomalies are disclosed

If evidence remains incomplete, designate:

**DMW One to Watch**

This distinction should not automatically guarantee inclusion in the final 100.

---

# 17. Temporary Closures and Renovations

If a ranked hotel closes temporarily:

* Retain its historical ranking within the relevant edition
* Clearly label the closure on its profile
* Do not present live booking actions
* Reassess it before inclusion in the next edition

If a major renovation fundamentally changes the product:

* Treat the renewed property as requiring a substantial reassessment
* Preserve the prior edition’s historical record
* Add a Revisited Field Report where possible

---

# 18. Pricing Methodology Requirements

Pricing observations must record:

* Currency
* Room category or room basis
* Number of guests
* Refundability
* Breakfast inclusion
* Tax inclusion
* Observation date
* Stay date
* Minimum-stay conditions
* Availability status
* Source or collection method

Pricing comparisons must:

* Use reasonably comparable room products
* Use consistent occupancy assumptions
* Explain tax and currency treatment
* Avoid comparing promotional suites with standard rooms
* Identify major event periods
* Avoid treating a single price observation as an annual pricing strategy

The annual price curve should ideally use:

* The lowest available qualifying room
* One-night stays
* Two adults or one consistent occupancy basis
* Flexible or consistently defined cancellation terms
* A common collection date or documented collection window

If methodology varies, disclose it.

---

# 19. Special Distinctions

Special distinctions provide nuance beyond the numerical ranking.

Available distinctions include:

* Best for Business Travel
* Pricing Power
* Hospitality Ecosystem
* Independent Excellence
* Urban Resort
* Best Repositioning
* Best New Hotel
* Best Hotel Restaurant
* Most Investable Hospitality Concept
* DMW One to Watch

A hotel may receive more than one distinction internally, but normally display no more than three prominently.

Distinctions must have a written rationale.

They cannot be purchased.

---

# 20. Annual Review Process

Recommended process:

## Stage 1: Candidate universe

Create a longlist of approximately 250–300 hotels.

## Stage 2: Structured research

Complete the canonical hotel record.

## Stage 3: Preliminary scoring

Score each dimension and assign confidence.

## Stage 4: Pricing analysis

Collect representative pricing data and define the relevant competitive set.

## Stage 5: Editorial review

Review scores, evidence quality, contradictions and possible adjustments.

## Stage 6: Portfolio review

Check for research bias, category omissions and unjustified concentration.

## Stage 7: Final ranking

Lock ranking, distinctions and DMW judgements.

## Stage 8: Publication

Publish the edition while allowing permanent profiles to receive factual updates.

---

# 21. Update Policy

Between annual editions, DMW may update:

* Hotel facts
* Ownership or operator information
* Amenities
* Booking links
* Pricing charts
* Field Reports
* Strategic articles
* Closure or renovation notices

Do not silently change an edition’s official rank.

A change in rank should occur through:

* A new annual edition
* A clearly labelled correction of factual or calculation error

---

# 22. Public Methodology Versus Internal Data

Publicly display:

* Ranking dimensions
* Weights
* Scoring philosophy
* Evidence statuses
* Research process
* Commercial independence
* Annual update process

Keep internal unless editorially required:

* Draft dimension scores
* Internal source-quality comments
* Sensitive ownership observations
* Unpublished editorial debate
* Personal information
* Adjustment discussions
* Unverified allegations

---

# 23. Prototype Ranking Warning

Until the initial comparison group has been researched and scored, all displayed ranks are illustrative.

The prototype must visibly state:

> The displayed Version 1 ranking is a design and methodology demonstration. Final positions will be established following completion of the DMW Hotels 100 research and review process.

Do not present St Martins Lane’s illustrative rank as a final published judgement.

---

# 24. Calibration Questions

After scoring the initial comparison group, review:

* Does the model overreward large amenity ecosystems?
* Does it unfairly penalise compact urban hotels?
* Does it overreward expensive locations?
* Can an excellent business hotel compete with a resort?
* Does pricing power receive sufficient weight?
* Is service being scored too confidently without firsthand visits?
* Does design distinctiveness overpower operating performance?
* Is asset scarcity double-counted through location and pricing?
* Are independent hotels disadvantaged by lower public-data availability?
* Does the final ordering make strategic sense when read as a complete list?

Revise the methodology only after documenting the answers.

---

# 25. Governing Ranking Principle

The calculation creates discipline.

Evidence creates credibility.

Editorial judgement creates meaning.

The ranking should ultimately answer:

> Which hotels most successfully transform hospitality, brand, operations and real estate into an exceptional and enduring proposition?
